EXHIBIT B

Product vision & visual direction

Vision. Shadows of Judgment is a four-minute noir standoff played with one thumb: two people chase justice by opposite means, choose in secret, and learn together what it cost. The redesign makes that fantasy legible by turning eight abstract numbers into two physical ideas — locks you are prying open and fuses burning toward you — and by collapsing the win rule into two words a player already understands: SEAL and DOOM. Because both sides read the same four axes at opposite polarity, every row on the board is an argument: the number that saves you is the number that buries them. Nothing about the simultaneous-turn engine changes. We simply stop asking players to do arithmetic under time pressure and start letting them feel the score.

EXHIBIT C

Battle information architecture

Today the screen shows eight bars of equal weight and asks the player to work out what they mean. The redesign gives every band one job and one question, in this order.

1
AM I WINNING OR DYING?
SEAL and DOOM chips on both name rails. Two numbers per player, one green one red, biggest type in their band. Read in under a second, from the thumb rest.
2
WHY?
The Case Board — four axis rows, all eight values, mine left theirs right. Consulted, not scanned. This is where a player learns which lever to pull.
3
WHAT DO I DO?
Hand + one button, bottom 210px, inside thumb reach. Max four cards visible, horizontally scrollable, one primary action that changes label with phase.
DEMOTED TO ON-DEMAND
Full stat history · deck count · card catalogue · opponent energy history · settings · rules. All one tap away, none resident. The battle log becomes a sheet you pull, not a scrolling box that eats 90px forever.
HOW THE TWO WIN RULES ARE DRAWN
▲ TWO LOCKS — mode "all"
Both green stats must reach 100, so the binding number is the lower of the two. Show it as one figure: SEAL = min(g1, g2). A player pushing 100/40 instantly sees SEAL 40 and knows exactly which lock is stuck. The row itself shows both values; the chip shows the truth.
▼ TWO FUSES — mode "any"
Either red stat at 100 ends the match, so only the worst one matters: DOOM = max(r1, r2). At 70 the chip turns amber, at 85 it turns red and breathes on a 1.1s loop. That single behaviour is the whole "who is in danger" problem, solved.
seal = Math.min(...character.positiveStats.map(k => stats[k]))
doom = Math.max(...character.negativeStats.map(k => stats[k]))
// both arrays already exist in characters.js — no data changes
Ten words of copy carry the rest, permanently, under the board: ▲ LOCK · CLIMB TO 100 and ▼ FUSE · DIES AT 100. There is no rules screen to forget.

EFFECT LANGUAGE SYSTEM
GRAMMAR
[±VALUE] [STAT] [▸THEM?] on the card face; [±VALUE] [your|their] [Full Stat Label] in the modal. Never a verb — verbs are what made the old descriptions unscannable.
COLOUR
Green if the change helps you, red if it hurts you — computed, never authored. Raising their fuse is green on your card; dropping your own lock is red. One rule, no exceptions, works for both characters from the same data.
SIGN
Always explicit: +12 / −8. The arrow direction and the colour are independent channels, which is what makes it colour-blind safe.
ORDER
Sorted by absolute magnitude, descending. Biggest consequence first, every card, every time.
TRUNCATION
Two lines on the face, then +N MORE. Cards with four effects stop lying about being simple.
FLAVOUR
Demoted to the modal, set in marker script, in quotes. It is voice, not information.

RISKS & MITIGATIONS
The Death Note lineage. An unlicensed TTF plus lCards/kiraCards and "L or Kira" in the GDD is a real problem the moment you charge money or run a Kickstarter. Mitigation: ship the OFL marker face, rename the deck exports to detectiveCards/vigilanteCards and the assets to detective/vigilante now, while it is a rename and not a migration. Keep the tone, drop the trademarks.
SEAL/DOOM could feel abstract. Two invented words are a bet. Mitigation: they are always adjacent to the real labels and the /100 denominator, and the tutorial names them in steps 1–2. If testers stumble, fall back to "BOTH LOCKS 44" and "WORST FUSE 62" — same maths, longer strings.
Vertical budget on small phones. 667 CSS px is tight with seven bands. Mitigation: the table band is the only flexible one (flex:1, 150–190px). Below 640px it drops first; below 600px the case board collapses row labels to glyphs. Nothing else moves.
Multiplayer timing. A 1.4s reveal must be identical on both phones or the drama breaks. Mitigation: server sends a single resolution payload with a timestamp; both clients animate from it. Never animate from local prediction.
Scope creep into deckbuilding. Mitigation: none of this document requires new cards or new mechanics. Ship the read of the game before expanding the game.
